Mixed arm (top-m exact + (k-m) approximation) MEASURED on smol + layer-local real K3

The mixed arm interpolates between two extremes. At one extreme, all top-k experts are computed exactly (high quality, but you must stream all k experts from NVMe). At the other, D3 replaces the whole routed bank with one shared MLP (cheap, but ~30% error). The mixed arm picks a split point m — compute the top-m exactly, use D3 for the remaining (k−m). The surprising finding is that PPL actually improves at m=1/m=2 vs original: D3 acts as a regularizer on the noisy tail experts.

The problem the mixed arm solves. A routed MoE layer picks top-k experts per token (k=4 on smol, k=16 on K3) with weights w₁ ≥ w₂ ≥ … ≥ wₖ. At K3 scale, computing all k experts exactly means streaming ~1 GB of expert weights per token per layer — infeasible. But replacing all of them with D3 leaves ~30% per-layer error. The mixed arm lets you pay for the few experts that matter most and approximate the rest.

The algorithm, step by step

For each token, at each converted layer:

# Inputs: latent (token's hidden state), indices (top-k expert ids), weights (w₁..wₖ), m (split point) # Three pieces:
# 1. EXACT (the top-m heavy hitters): exact_top_m = Σᵢ₌₁..m wᵢ · expert_i(latent) # uses real expert weights
# 2. D3 FULL (D3's prediction for ALL k experts): d3_full = D3(latent, indices, weights)
# 3. D3 TOP-m ONLY (D3's prediction for just the top-m, with bottom weights zeroed): bot_weights = weights.clone(); bot_weights[:, :m] = 0 d3_top_only = D3(latent, indices, bot_weights)
# Combine: top-m from exact, the rest from D3 d3_bot_contrib = d3_full − d3_top_only # isolates D3's prediction for the bottom k−m mixed_output = exact_top_m + d3_bot_contrib

Why it works — three observations

1. The top expert carries disproportionate information

On smol, top-1 carries about 35% of routing mass. On the real K3 layer, computing only the highest-weight expert exactly lifts held-out cosine from 0.62475 to 0.84923. That is a large gain for 1/16 of exact routed work, though still far from drop-in fidelity.

2. D3's error decomposes additively

D3's output is shared_core · (1 + Σ wᵢ·scaleᵢ) + Σ wᵢ·shiftᵢ. Each expert's contribution is linear in its weight. Zeroing the top-m weights cleanly isolates D3's prediction for the bottom experts via simple subtraction (d3_full − d3_top_m_only) — no re-training, no special architecture.

3. The firebreak contains the residual

Even at m=0 (pure D3), smol's AttnRes firebreak at every-4-layers resets the residual stream so per-layer D3 error doesn't compound catastrophically (scattered KL = 0.017). With m≥1, per-layer error drops further and the firebreak has even less to contain.

Surprising finding — PPL improves at m=1/m=2 vs ORIGINAL. On smol: ORIGINAL PPL = 4.64, Mixed m=1 PPL = 4.56, Mixed m=2 PPL = 4.58. D3 acts as a regularizer on the tail experts. The bottom-k experts in the original model introduce noise (they're selected by the router but contribute little); D3's smoothed prediction produces slightly cleaner outputs than the original experts themselves. Adding back top-m exact recovers the signal while keeping D3's regularization on the tail.
