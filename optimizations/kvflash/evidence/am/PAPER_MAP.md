## Is "context-prefill" (cell F) a named thing in the paper?

Yes, by exactly that name: Sec 3.1 defines it in two sentences inside the "Repeat-prefill" paragraph as the "simpler variant of running a prefill on C alone", attributes it to H2O, says it is cheaper (Table 3: 7 s vs 8 s repeat-prefill vs 139 s self-study on Gemma 60k) but "slightly worse" than repeat-prefill. App D / Fig 6 plots it among eight sampling variants with log-perplexity; the text there says self-study is best and "repeat and context-prefill close behind". It is not one of the paper's four named AM variants and is not in Fig 4.

Difference from the other two, as extracted:
- **context-prefill**: one forward pass over the article alone. Queries are the article tokens' own queries (`context_prefill.py` L94-135, article tokens only via the index range L51). No instruction, no second copy of C, no generation.
- **repeat (repeat-prefill)**: prefill of article plus the instruction plus a second copy of the article as the "answer". Queries are taken from the instruction and the second copy ("query activations starting from the instruction through the second C"). Teacher-forced, no sampling. This is what the model computes when it copies the document, so it sees a query distribution closer to generation than context-prefill does (queries in the second copy attend back to the first copy).
- **self-study**: the model generates conversations about C (fixed prompts such as summarize/aggregate/structure_json, plus 3 generated questions; Table 8, App C.4) and the queries come from those conversations. It needs vLLM sampling (139 s in Table 3) and adds randomness (our seed 1). `ss-plus-repeat` is repeat plus four self-study prompts (`ss-plus-repeat.py`), capped at 50,000 vectors per KV head.

## (a) How the per-head budget is computed

- **Signal.** Sec 3.4 and App E: per-head sensitivity (influence) curves. For one head at a time, change that head's compaction ratio from 0 up to a maximum while every other head stays at a uniform baseline (0.05 in the paper, "baseline schedule uniform" in the script), and record the change in loss. The loss is the mean log-perplexity of reference answers (greedy answers from the uncompacted model to the article's questions) under the compacted cache (`head_budget_optimization/influence.py` L613-634: `delta_log_ppl = avg_log_ppl - baseline_log_perplexity`; reference answers `evaluation/utils.py` L907; perplexity `L1229`). Queries for the fit use `ss-plus-repeat` and the method `highest_attn_keys_rms_nnls2_-3_3_lsq` in the paper's script (`scripts/head-budget-optimization.sh`, Gemma example: articles 90-99, 51 eval points, `--max-ratio 0.5`, solve ratios 0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1). Curves are averaged over 10 QuALITY articles (Fig 3 caption; `influence.py` L817 `aggregate_head_curves`).
- **Solver.** Paper Alg 4 is greedy exchange from a uniform allocation at reference ratio r0 = 0.05: swap a step of share between heads while the summed curve loss falls (separability assumption). The repo default is a variant: `--solver-method ratio-agnostic` (`run.py`, default; saves `optimized_agnostic.json` at L549), `solver.py` L1017 `solve_ratio_agnostic_swap` and L1161 `solve_ratio_agnostic`. It swaps proportion steps (0.001) between heads to minimise the weighted mean loss over several target ratios at once (default 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, uniform weights; `compute_loss_at_ratio` L890). So the paper's Alg 4 (one r0) and the shipped file's procedure (multi-ratio) are not identical; I could not recover the exact settings used for the shipped Qwen3-4B file from the repo.
- **"Agnostic".** Yes. "optimized_agnostic" means agnostic to the target ratio (one proportion vector for all ratios) and, because curves are averaged over articles, agnostic to the input. It is model-specific. The paper states the same property in Sec 3.4: "reused across contexts and compaction ratios", backed by the claim that head sensitivity rankings are stable across inputs (Fig 3, Fig 7).
- **Normalisation.** The file stores proportions p_h with sum 1 (`convert_to_proportions`, `solver.py` L623). Per-head keys = `int(p_h * S * H)` where S = `int(article_tokens * target)` and H = number of compacted heads (global layers x KV heads) (`per_layer_head_on_policy.py` L258; `per_layer_head.py` L313-316). Heads missing from the file get S (L260). Total over heads is conserved at S x H up to integer truncation.
- **`max_ratio_per_head`.** Not in the paper. Repo flag (`base.py` L208-302, `apply_max_ratio_cap`): the largest allowed proportion is `max_ratio_per_head / (target_ratio * total_heads)`; any head above it is clamped to that value and the excess is redistributed to the uncapped heads in proportion to their shares (water-filling, loop to total_heads). At 0.75 no head may retain more than 75% of the article's tokens. It binds on the 4B (288 heads; our check: yes) and does not bind on the 27B (64 heads, max proportion 0.0866 vs cap 0.117 at 0.10).

## (b) How the compacted cache is stored and masked

- **beta.** Stored as a (B, KV, t) tensor per layer next to C1 (keys) and C2 (values) (`models/cache.py` L15-33, `beta_for_layer` L213). At attention, beta is expanded to query heads and ADDED to the attention mask over the first `prefix_length` key positions: `modified_attention_mask[:, :, :, :prefix_length] += beta_heads.unsqueeze(2)` (`models/qwen3/modeling_qwen3.py` L223-260). This is the paper's Eq (1) exp(q Ck^T + beta), implemented as an additive bias in SDPA (paper Sec 2: "supported in common attention implementations such as PyTorch SDPA"). New tokens get beta = 0 (`models/generate.py` L459-476).
- **Padding.** Each head is compacted to its own t_h. Heads that return fewer than asked (OMP `drop_key_beta_cutoff` can run out) are padded to t_h with K = 0, V = 0, beta = -inf (`per_layer_head_on_policy.py` L470-490). Then all heads of a layer are padded to the layer's longest head the same way (L570-590; `per_layer_head.py` L637-657). The paper only notes the naive padded layout in a footnote of Sec 3.4 ("pad all heads to the length of the longest KV-head, increasing the effective context length") and says varlen kernels avoid it; the -inf mechanism is the repo's own.
- **Softmax.** A padded slot's logit is q . 0 + (-inf) = -inf, so exp = 0: it contributes nothing to numerator or denominator and the head's attention equals the unpadded head's. A row of all -inf would give NaN, but every row also attends the kept prompt tokens and new tokens, and `nan_layers` is empty in every R1b row. So padding costs memory and bandwidth, not accuracy.
- **Slot count.** Confirmed: attended slots per head = per-head budget plus kept non-article tokens, and the budgets sum to S x H (int truncation only, ratio 0.9995 in our recompute). So attended mean = S + 25 = 680.2 of 6560.9 article tokens = 10.37% at target 0.10 on the 27B (5.37% at 0.05). The padded tensor is the per-layer maximum head: 1115.55 = 17.0% (`full_layer_len_mean`). Our `R1b_budget_and_baseline_check.md` result is therefore correct, not an anomaly. The repo itself tracks both numbers: `tensor_article_tokens` (padded) vs `effective_article_tokens` (excludes -inf padding) and reports the compaction ratio from the effective one (`evaluation/qa_evaluator.py` L1405-1434, `per_layer_head_on_policy.py` L611-624). Caveat for OMP cells (C, O2): heads that return fewer keys than their budget are masked further, so their attended count is at most 680.
